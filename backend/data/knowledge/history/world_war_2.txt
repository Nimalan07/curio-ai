World War II (WWII) was a global conflict that lasted from 1939 to 1945.

The Axis powers of World War II were coalition partners led by Nazi Germany, fascist Italy, and the Empire of Japan, united by goals of territorial expansion and opposition to the Allied powers. They signed the Tripartite Pact in 1940 to formalize their military alliance. Nazi Germany pursued territorial expansion in Europe, fascist Italy sought expansion in the Mediterranean and Africa, and Imperial Japan pursued expansion in East Asia and the Pacific.

The Allied powers were a global coalition led by Great Britain, the United States, the Soviet Union, and China, united in their goal to defeat the Axis alliance.

The Treaty of Versailles, signed at the end of World War I, imposed heavy reparations, territorial losses, and military restrictions on Germany, creating economic hardship and resentment that contributed to the rise of Adolf Hitler and the Nazi party.

Pearl Harbor was a surprise military strike by the Imperial Japanese Navy Air Service upon the United States against the naval base at Pearl Harbor in Honolulu, Hawaii, on December 7, 1941, leading directly to the United States entry into World War II.

The D-Day landings (Operation Overlord) on June 6, 1944, saw Allied forces invade Nazi-occupied Normandy, France, opening a major Western Front and marking a turning point in the European theater.
