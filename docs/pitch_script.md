# Curio — Hackathon Pitch Script (2 Minutes)

## 0:00 - 0:15 | The Problem
> "Most educational AI behaves like an answer key. When students get stuck, the AI gives them the answer, creating an illusion of competence. However, cognitive science shows we only truly understand something when we explain it to someone else."

## 0:15 - 0:35 | Introducing Curio
> "Meet Curio. Instead of teaching you, Curio is an AI student that you have to teach. Built using React, FastAPI, and local Llama 3.1 via Ollama, Curio is private, secure, and has zero running costs."

## 0:35 - 1:05 | Topic Selector & The Confidence Slider
> "We select a topic like 'Photosynthesis' and set our initial confidence rating to 8 out of 10. We begin teaching. We explain that plants make food using sunlight, carbon dioxide, and water. Instead of a generic follow-up, Curio analyzes this statement and asks: 'What happens to the sunlight after the plant captures it?' targeting our missing steps."

## 1:05 - 1:15 | Why I Asked This
> "If we're unsure why Curio is asking, we click 'Why I asked this' to see the AI's internal reasoning: 'Your explanation jumps from sunlight to glucose without explaining the intermediate energy-transfer process.' This adds complete transparency to active learning."

## 1:15 - 1:35 | Understanding Report & Confidence Gap
> "After a few turns, we generate the Understanding Report. Curio plots our scores across Clarity, Completeness, Accuracy, and Depth. It reveals our **Confidence Gap**: we rated our confidence as 8/10, but our explanation scored 6.8/10. That 1.2-point gap is where true learning happens."

## 1:35 - 1:52 | Actionable Insights & Download
> "Curio outlines exactly what we explained well, our gaps, possible misconceptions, and what to review next. We can download this complete report card as a PNG for offline tracking."

## 1:52 - 2:00 | Future Roadmap
> "In the future, we're bringing Curio to voice-based viva learning, progress history over time, and teacher dashboards to identify class-wide misconceptions. Don't just study it. Teach it to Curio."
